01 — Vision

A paradigm shift toward
open, reproducible orbital analysis.

The GOOD platform transforms orbital dynamics from a closed field of fragmented, domain-specific tools into an accessible, collaborative effort — supporting professional scientists, engineers, and citizen scientists alike.

i.

Run & archive

Execute new analyses on the platform — locally or via hosted compute — then validate and store them with full provenance.

ii.

Retrieve, reproducibly

Pull existing analyses back — every input, configuration, and output reconstructable from persistent identifiers.

iii.

Auto-configure

Generate skeleton analysis configurations from a few user inputs: target body, model accuracy, required datasets.

iv.

Stay current

Archived analyses update automatically as new data and functionality become available — no user action needed.

02 — Why now

Closed tools, growing data, urgent questions.

Orbital dynamics underpins planetary interior modelling, fundamental-physics tests, spacecraft operations, and planetary defense. Yet the tools to do it are largely closed-source — and the data is multiplying.

The problem

  • Conflicting findings are difficult to resolve when underlying code is opaque.
  • Unexpected results can't be independently validated by the broader community.
  • Cross-domain collaboration is hindered by fragmented, domain-specific tools.
  • Innovation is restricted by limited access to high-fidelity infrastructure.

What's driving urgency

  • Crowded near-Earth orbit demands FAIR, standardized space situational awareness.
  • Planetary mission quantity & quality require consistent fusion of multi-mission data.
  • Asteroid data diversity from Gaia, LSST, Euclid, JWST and orbiters demands interoperable models.
  • Strategic autonomy in Europe needs open alternatives to closed, centralised systems.
03 — How it works

Two proven systems,
one open platform.

GOOD brings together Tudat's high-fidelity orbit propagation and estimation with WISE's mature, lineage-preserving data infrastructure.

TU Delft

Tudat

The TU Delft Astrodynamics Toolbox: an open-source C++ / Python suite for numerical propagation and estimation of spacecraft and natural solar system bodies. Central to the TU Delft Spaceflight M.Sc. and ~40 peer-reviewed papers.

BSD 3-clause licensed, distributed as conda packages, with ~300 automated tests.

View on GitHub
University of Groningen

WISE

A scientific information system born from Astro-WISE, now underpinning the NOVA Long Term Archive and the Euclid Data Processing System. Preserves full data lineage in a rich data model — 2,500+ Euclid users today.

Hosted at OmegaCEN ASDC, part of the University of Groningen's data infrastructure.

04 — Applications

From deep interiors to debris orbits.

The same methodology — fitting data to dynamical models — connects planetary science, fundamental physics, and space situational awareness. GOOD makes that methodology shareable.

α

Planetary interiors

Reproducible modelling of solar system body interior, origin, and evolution — including icy moon habitability.

β

Planetary defense

Open estimation and prediction for Near-Earth Object impact monitoring and spacecraft conjunction analysis.

γ

Small bodies

Large-scale analysis of Earth- and space-based data for asteroid evolution and fundamental physics tests.

δ

Citizen science

Observation calibration, science-optimized data acquisition, and community-contributed datasets.
